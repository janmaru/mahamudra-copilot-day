# AI Usage in Italian Companies — Context and Policy Gap

Based on recent research, there is plenty of aggregated data and contextual evidence, but virtually no source names specific Italian companies that "know" their employees use AI yet have no formal policy. The topic is treated at a statistical and regulatory level, not at the level of identifiable individual companies.

## What the available data shows

- A Salesforce survey reported by **LineaEDP** indicates that in Italy **17%** of surveyed workers use GenAI tools at work, and about **49%** do so without formal employer approval.
- In the same survey, **42%** of Italian workers state that their company has **no AI policy at all**, while only **15%** work in environments with well-defined guidelines.
- An analysis cited by **key4biz** reports that **73%** of Italian workers use generative AI **without a corporate strategy**, i.e. without a clear organizational and governance framework — a sign that many companies effectively *tolerate* AI use but do not regulate it.

In practice, the picture is:

- a non-trivial share of employees use AI at work;
- employers are often aware, or at least "turn a blind eye";
- in many cases there is no formalized policy or explicit strategy.

## Why you do not find company names

- Available research is based on **worker interviews** and does not ask for the employer's name, so results are deliberately anonymous.
- From a legal and reputational standpoint, no serious outlet would publish "Company X knows its employees use AI but has no policy", as this would be a potentially defamatory claim unless backed by internal documents.

If this is needed for a thesis, article, or consulting work, the standard approach is:

1. Cite **Salesforce / LineaEDP** data to quantify unauthorized use and absence of policy in Italy.
2. Add the **73%** figure on workers using GenAI without a corporate strategy.
3. Connect these numbers to emerging obligations (**AI Act + Italian law on algorithms in HR**) to argue that many companies are currently in a "grey zone" of awareness without formal governance.

## Regulation that makes the absence of a policy relevant

- The **AI Act (Reg. EU 2024/1689)** requires more structured governance of high-risk systems, including those affecting HR processes, with obligations on transparency, data quality, human oversight, and auditing.
- In Italy, **Law 132/2025** introduced the employer's obligation to clearly inform workers and unions about AI systems used in personnel management processes, with administrative sanctions for omissions.
- Several doctrinal commentaries stress that internal AI policies effectively become a **necessary tool** to comply with these obligations and to responsibly manage legal, privacy, and discrimination risks.
